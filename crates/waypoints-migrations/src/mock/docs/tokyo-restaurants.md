## Tsukiji / Toyosu Area

The outer market at Tsukiji is still the best spot for a seafood breakfast. A few standouts:

- **Sushi Dai** — legendary, but the queue starts at 5 AM and waits can be 3+ hours
- **Daiwa Sushi** — right next door, nearly as good, shorter line
- **Tamagoyaki stalls** — grab a stick of the sweet rolled omelette while you walk

> If you're into the wholesale side, the actual tuna auction moved to Toyosu Market. You need to book a viewing slot in advance.

## Ramen Worth the Detour

Tokyo has an _absurd_ density of ramen shops. These are the ones worth planning around:

1. **Fuunji** (Shinjuku) — tsukemen specialist. The dipping broth is intensely rich. Expect a 20-minute wait at lunch.
2. **Afuri** (Ebisu) — yuzu shio ramen. Light, citrusy, completely different from heavy tonkotsu styles.
3. **Rokurinsha** (Tokyo Station) — another tsukemen legend, located inside Ramen Street in the station basement.
4. **Nakiryu** (Otsuka) — Michelin-starred tantanmen. Spicy sesame broth, `¥1,000` for a bowl.

### Ramen Ordering Tips

Most ramen shops use a **ticket machine** (食券機) at the entrance. The flow:

- Insert cash (most don't take cards)
- Press the button for your order — the top-left button is usually the house special
- Hand the ticket to the chef
- Specify noodle firmness if asked: `カタメ` (firm), `フツウ` (normal), `ヤワラカメ` (soft)

## Izakaya Picks

For evening drinking and small plates:

- **Torikizoku** — chain izakaya, everything is ¥350. Great for budget nights.
- **Uoharu** (Roppongi) — upscale seafood izakaya. The sashimi platter is stunning.
- **Tachinomi** (standing bars in Yurakucho) — under the train tracks, as atmospheric as it gets. ~~Tourist trap~~ Genuinely beloved by salarymen.

Don't skip **yakitori** alleys in Yurakucho and Shinjuku Memory Lane (Omoide Yokocho). Order by the stick: `ねぎま` (chicken and leek), `つくね` (meatball), `かわ` (skin — crispy perfection).
