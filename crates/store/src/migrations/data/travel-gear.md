## The Carry-On Setup

After way too many trips with checked bags, I've gone full **one-bag travel**. Here's the current loadout:

- **Bag**: Osprey Farpoint 40L — fits overhead on budget airlines, hip belt stows away
- **Packing cubes**: 3 × Eagle Creek compression cubes. Game-changer for organization.
- **Dopp kit**: minimal — solid shampoo bar, toothbrush, sunscreen. Buy the rest locally.

### Electronics

- Laptop + charger (USB-C, doubles for phone)
- Kindle Paperwhite — _essential_ for long train rides
- AirPods + over-ear headphones (Sony WH-1000XM5 for flights)
- Universal power adapter — the `Epicka` all-in-one handles Japan's Type A plugs and EU/UK too

> Tip: Japan runs on 100V. Most modern chargers handle 100–240V automatically, but check the fine print on hair dryers and curling irons.

## Clothing Strategy

The key principle: **merino wool base layers**. They regulate temperature, resist odor for multiple days, and dry fast when hand-washed.

My 14-day loadout for spring:

1. 3 × merino t-shirts (rotate and wash)
2. 1 × merino long-sleeve
3. 1 × lightweight down jacket (packable)
4. 1 × rain shell
5. 2 × quick-dry pants (one doubles as hiking pants)
6. 1 × shorts
7. 5 × merino underwear
8. 3 × merino socks

Everything gets hand-washed in the sink every 3–4 days. Hotels in Japan often have coin laundry too.

## Apps and Tools

Software that makes travel smoother:

- **Google Maps** — offline maps of each city, downloaded in advance
- **Google Translate** — the camera mode reads Japanese signs in real-time
- **Suica app** — load your IC card from your phone (iPhone only in Japan)
- **Hyperdia** — train schedule lookup, better than Google for JR routes
- **Tabelog** — the _real_ restaurant rating app in Japan (3.5+ is genuinely excellent)

### Connectivity

Rent a **pocket Wi-Fi** from the airport. ~~eSIM works but coverage is spotty outside cities.~~ Actually, eSIM providers like Ubigi and Airalo have gotten much better — check recent reviews before defaulting to pocket Wi-Fi.
