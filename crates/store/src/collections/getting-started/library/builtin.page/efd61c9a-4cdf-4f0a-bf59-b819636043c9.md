## Tsukiji / Toyosu Area

<!-- wp:mark 7b9c1dd3-3963-4cc8-9d43-0d65935bebac -->
The outer market at Tsukiji is still the best spot for a seafood breakfast. A few standouts:

<!-- wp:mark b0349e99-dcee-4fd2-b8b5-a998067e7e7b -->
- *Sushi Dai* — legendary, but the queue starts at 5 AM and waits can be 3+ hours

<!-- wp:mark 7c3690d7-ba1b-4c20-b57e-8a0bf2509a9e -->
- *Daiwa Sushi* — right next door, nearly as good, shorter line

<!-- wp:mark 9a0f1d3a-bbc3-4710-8a4f-62f3c67be85d -->
- *Tamagoyaki stalls* — grab a stick of the sweet rolled omelette while you walk

<!-- wp:mark db5c3e5f-867f-4b89-a9f6-5c81113322d1 -->
> <!-- wp:mark f37cfbe8-1ffb-4019-831f-5b84edd67d8b -->
> If you're into the wholesale side, the actual tuna auction moved to Toyosu Market. You need to book a viewing slot in advance.

## Ramen Worth the Detour

<!-- wp:mark ce41246e-da3d-41a7-bc1a-6f958a02f937 -->
Tokyo has an _absurd_ density of ramen shops. These are the ones worth planning around:

<!-- wp:mark 4239a699-506f-48e8-ae0b-4cab038890d7 -->
+ *Fuunji* (Shinjuku) — tsukemen specialist. The dipping broth is intensely rich. Expect a 20-minute wait at lunch.

<!-- wp:mark 0d2dd9b0-53b0-4cf3-b7ea-1b7d2da71a70 -->
+ *Afuri* (Ebisu) — yuzu shio ramen. Light, citrusy, completely different from heavy tonkotsu styles.

<!-- wp:mark d949e4e1-8436-4d9f-9b9c-34eedd79a535 -->
+ *Rokurinsha* (Tokyo Station) — another tsukemen legend, located inside Ramen Street in the station basement.

<!-- wp:mark 87ebd9e8-f5f4-49c3-9095-f4ee6d5b0dfa -->
+ *Nakiryu* (Otsuka) — Michelin-starred tantanmen. Spicy sesame broth, `¥1,000` for a bowl.

<!-- wp:mark 2ea029ac-49cc-4a10-8361-a13fba307c27 -->
### Ramen Ordering Tips

<!-- wp:mark 7a8e61c5-5c08-4ea6-8e81-4c3addf62a53 -->
Most ramen shops use a *ticket machine* (食券機) at the entrance. The flow:

<!-- wp:mark 9fcefaac-cea7-4a8a-8ba6-1a9803285212 -->
- Insert cash (most don't take cards)

<!-- wp:mark 1bd304ce-6d16-45b4-aa45-48c38b6af191 -->
- Press the button for your order — the top-left button is usually the house special

<!-- wp:mark 55039052-6a71-49f4-b565-40a896a4569f -->
- Hand the ticket to the chef

<!-- wp:mark cf5c2478-89bb-460b-b221-2db43c2343e1 -->
- Specify noodle firmness if asked: `カタメ` (firm), `フツウ` (normal), `ヤワラカメ` (soft)

## Izakaya Picks

<!-- wp:mark dc01e61e-d2d1-4cd2-910e-db3e9eee3406 -->
For evening drinking and small plates:

<!-- wp:mark 113a24fc-766d-4e95-a889-bd3068ff3b59 -->
- *Torikizoku* — chain izakaya, everything is ¥350. Great for budget nights.

<!-- wp:mark 8fc997ce-ac71-431d-a7f4-b1f5ab4cd6b4 -->
- *Uoharu* (Roppongi) — upscale seafood izakaya. The sashimi platter is stunning.

<!-- wp:mark 86eb9392-916c-4aa4-9e85-c3a0a4497a55 -->
- *Tachinomi* (standing bars in Yurakucho) — under the train tracks, as atmospheric as it gets. ~~Tourist trap~~ Genuinely beloved by salarymen.

<!-- wp:mark 1f608aab-c7a5-4ace-ab85-2fa5415a2d94 -->
Don't skip *yakitori* alleys in Yurakucho and Shinjuku Memory Lane (Omoide Yokocho). Order by the stick: `ねぎま` (chicken and leek), `つくね` (meatball), `かわ` (skin — crispy perfection).
