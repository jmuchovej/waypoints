## Currently Reading

<!-- wp:mark 2f37fabf-4247-435b-9f13-79e2f02ad943 -->
[[The Order of Time]] by [[Carlo Rovelli]]. Short, poetic, and occasionally baffling. Rovelli argues that most of what we think we know about time is wrong — there is no universal "now," the distinction between past and future is thermodynamic, and at the quantum level time _might not exist at all_.

<!-- wp:mark 0084d8df-ded3-4f52-a01b-5958592e95c1 -->
I'm about halfway through. The chapter on thermal time is dense but fascinating.

## Reading Notes Format

<!-- wp:mark 505d3152-9037-4ea6-8c3c-ddbb95cc522c -->
I've been experimenting with how to take notes on non-fiction:

<!-- wp:mark 05f57330-e606-48d2-8adf-475f651dd982 -->
- *One page per chapter* — summary + 2–3 key ideas + my reactions

<!-- wp:mark 036e6bb6-b36d-46cb-9ce5-358ce796c7ed -->
- *Capture direct quotes* with page numbers for later reference

<!-- wp:mark 61dd9cde-f899-4527-9151-0ec10aba79bf -->
- *Link ideas across books* — e.g., Rovelli's thermal time connects to Boltzmann's entropy, which connects to information theory

<!-- wp:mark c670964e-5328-4673-a338-f985d44e1d55 -->
The goal isn't to transcribe the book. It's to build a web of _connected ideas_ that I can navigate later. Waypoints should be perfect for this once cross-references are working.
