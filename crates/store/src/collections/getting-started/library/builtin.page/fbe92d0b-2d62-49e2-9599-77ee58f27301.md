## The Carry-On Setup

<!-- wp:mark f59d7c59-99d1-46bc-947f-1504a0666ca5 -->
After way too many trips with checked bags, I've gone full *one-bag travel*. Here's the current loadout:

<!-- wp:mark 31f079e0-db0e-4521-a20c-8dd0d1961196 -->
- *Bag*: Osprey Farpoint 40L — fits overhead on budget airlines, hip belt stows away

<!-- wp:mark 0da3c331-aa3f-4655-b8eb-7e24e472728c -->
- *Packing cubes*: 3 × Eagle Creek compression cubes. Game-changer for organization.

<!-- wp:mark ef1b8f34-81b0-4807-bac3-775f53ccd2d6 -->
- *Dopp kit*: minimal — solid shampoo bar, toothbrush, sunscreen. Buy the rest locally.

<!-- wp:mark c9440d54-ad32-4d5d-8715-93ee7756dd84 -->
### Electronics

<!-- wp:mark e7efb013-f05a-4a8b-badc-18666f240d01 -->
- Laptop + charger (USB-C, doubles for phone)

<!-- wp:mark 54b060e5-468f-41b3-80d6-4d53adb175a3 -->
- Kindle Paperwhite — _essential_ for long train rides

<!-- wp:mark c8756829-7f5b-4882-9a66-5aa200865098 -->
- AirPods + over-ear headphones (Sony WH-1000XM5 for flights)

<!-- wp:mark eda01492-9274-4cf3-bc11-e1d4ad0a2926 -->
- Universal power adapter — the `Epicka` all-in-one handles Japan's Type A plugs and EU/UK too

<!-- wp:mark ea7ca0ac-c0b2-4654-bc7b-e32ce425efb0 -->
> <!-- wp:mark 85851009-4d93-43a6-ae90-1ef1e84b82b2 -->
> Tip: Japan runs on 100V. Most modern chargers handle 100–240V automatically, but check the fine print on hair dryers and curling irons.

## Clothing Strategy

<!-- wp:mark fe1d9410-f9f7-4746-a55d-c10d0cdd1790 -->
The key principle: *merino wool base layers*. They regulate temperature, resist odor for multiple days, and dry fast when hand-washed.

<!-- wp:mark acda35d2-1467-4161-afb4-a5ef8ed69ba3 -->
My 14-day loadout for spring:

<!-- wp:mark 9882d502-a749-4416-bc13-437327145619 -->
+ 3 × merino t-shirts (rotate and wash)

<!-- wp:mark 9a5b2b7f-a560-4e19-a522-61568eef2b8a -->
+ 1 × merino long-sleeve

<!-- wp:mark a08308cd-edc8-4c48-9c78-5e20d8bea59c -->
+ 1 × lightweight down jacket (packable)

<!-- wp:mark 5e81c543-a44e-4c00-bb3d-62f6b9d07e00 -->
+ 1 × rain shell

<!-- wp:mark 1efe55c0-28d6-45d4-9df7-89000411adf4 -->
+ 2 × quick-dry pants (one doubles as hiking pants)

<!-- wp:mark 3df4bafc-d9da-4772-994b-1f2653361023 -->
+ 1 × shorts

<!-- wp:mark 8d5a28aa-d894-46f9-8677-f0b80d72098b -->
+ 5 × merino underwear

<!-- wp:mark 66ca5756-0025-43b0-a433-3cb588edf056 -->
+ 3 × merino socks

<!-- wp:mark 4deb7df3-203d-4962-9438-38b1bad1ed95 -->
Everything gets hand-washed in the sink every 3–4 days. Hotels in Japan often have coin laundry too.

## Apps and Tools

<!-- wp:mark 07398aa8-795a-4230-96f2-3347a65bf6c1 -->
Software that makes travel smoother:

<!-- wp:mark 1bab2a4b-e30d-4e58-af14-b28d4baa4572 -->
- *Google Maps* — offline maps of each city, downloaded in advance

<!-- wp:mark 27938638-db90-46c2-8167-b7873255df42 -->
- *Google Translate* — the camera mode reads Japanese signs in real-time

<!-- wp:mark 26d22acc-afd8-4bf0-b4b7-e45b98c30e95 -->
- *Suica app* — load your IC card from your phone (iPhone only in Japan)

<!-- wp:mark 2371a43d-3337-49c8-8ed6-bca5f747f6b0 -->
- *Hyperdia* — train schedule lookup, better than Google for JR routes

<!-- wp:mark 7859f4aa-cdb0-4859-be1d-339f8370867e -->
- *Tabelog* — the _real_ restaurant rating app in Japan (3.5+ is genuinely excellent)

<!-- wp:mark bd98d841-e19c-4abd-8ce9-37c3ada12833 -->
