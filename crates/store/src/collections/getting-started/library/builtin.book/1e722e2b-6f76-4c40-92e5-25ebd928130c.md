A slim, careful walkthrough of the incompleteness theorems that manages to be both accessible and honest about the details. Nagel and Newman don't skip the hard parts — they build up from basic logic through Godel numbering to the self-referential construction — but they move at a pace that doesn't lose you.

<!-- wp:mark f1a14e1c-a3c0-4c36-b155-8c65d88f7d9d -->
This is the book I'd hand someone who wants to understand what Godel actually proved rather than the pop-science version ("math is broken!"). The distinction between the first and second incompleteness theorems is handled cleanly, and the implications are stated without overreach.

<!-- wp:mark 4d0bedc7-5572-458d-b865-9f0e9f2caa1c -->
At under 120 pages it respects your time. Pairs well with GEB if you want the expansive, playful treatment afterward, but this one stands on its own as the clearest exposition of the proof I've encountered.
