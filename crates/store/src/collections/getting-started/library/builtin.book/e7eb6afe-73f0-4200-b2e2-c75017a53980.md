Less a memoir and more a collection of stories Feynman told at dinner parties, transcribed and lightly edited. Safecracking at Los Alamos, learning to draw, playing bongos in Brazil — the range is absurd. The through-line is curiosity as a lifestyle rather than a profession.

<!-- wp:mark 0bebfc35-d8da-44ec-84c5-2d1e852ccfd9 -->
Some of the anecdotes haven't aged perfectly, and Feynman's self-mythologizing can get thick. But the chapters about his approach to problem-solving are genuinely instructive. The bit about "learning the names of things versus understanding them" stuck with me for years.

<!-- wp:mark 34600d6b-6f20-4043-9003-b599c2e4b752 -->
It's a fast, entertaining read that accidentally says more about the culture of mid-century physics than most histories do on purpose. Pair it with Gleick's biography for a more grounded picture.
