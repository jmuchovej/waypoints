<!-- wp:mark efb4bbae-feb0-474c-a028-f56ee67ef032 -->
The best single-volume overview of complexity science I've found. Mitchell covers cellular automata, genetic algorithms, information theory, networks, and computation — all without assuming much prior knowledge. She manages to be rigorous without being dry, which is harder than it looks for this material.

<!-- wp:mark 1fb1b495-ec2c-40ce-8be1-10418abaa091 -->
What I appreciated most is that she's honest about where the field's ambitions outrun its results. Complexity science has a habit of promising grand unified theories and delivering interesting metaphors; Mitchell flags this tension directly rather than glossing over it.

<!-- wp:mark 7adf9b7f-ae71-4128-9b12-519bbf9d7eaa -->
The chapters on computation and dynamics are the strongest. The network science section felt slightly dated on re-read, but the foundations she lays are still solid. Good companion to Strogatz's Nonlinear Dynamics if you want to go deeper on the math side.
