The book that gave us "paradigm shift" and then watched helplessly as the term escaped into every corporate strategy deck. Kuhn's actual argument is more nuanced than the buzzword suggests: science doesn't progress by smooth accumulation but through periods of crisis where the old framework breaks down and a new one replaces it, often for reasons that aren't purely rational.

The historical case studies — Copernicus, Lavoisier, Einstein — are well chosen and still compelling. Kuhn's description of "normal science" as puzzle-solving within an accepted framework resonated with how I think about most engineering work too.

It's short but dense. The later chapters on incommensurability between paradigms are the most controversial and the most interesting. Whether or not you buy the strong version of his thesis, it permanently changes how you read scientific progress narratives.
