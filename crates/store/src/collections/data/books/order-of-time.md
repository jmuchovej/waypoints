A poetic exploration of the nature of time by a theoretical physicist. Rovelli dismantles our everyday intuitions about time — arguing there is no universal "now," that the flow of time is a thermodynamic effect, and that at the quantum level, time might not exist at all.

The first half reads almost like philosophy, grounding big claims in loop quantum gravity without drowning you in math. The second half gets more speculative and personal, tying the physics back to human experience and memory. Rovelli writes like someone who genuinely loves both Dante and Boltzmann in equal measure.

Short, elegant, and occasionally mind-bending. The chapter on thermal time is dense but rewards careful reading. This is the kind of book that changes how you think about a concept you thought you already understood.
