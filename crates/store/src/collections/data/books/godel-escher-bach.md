The book that ruined a whole generation of programmers by making them think consciousness is just a really fancy loop. Hofstadter weaves together formal systems, fugues, and Escher's impossible staircases into an argument about self-reference and the origins of meaning. It's ambitious to the point of being slightly unhinged.

The dialogues between Achilles and the Tortoise are the highlight — playful, recursive, and surprisingly effective at making abstract logic feel tangible. The chapters on formal number theory are slower going but essential scaffolding for the payoff around incompleteness.

I've read this twice now and gotten something completely different out of it each time. First pass was all about the math. Second pass I noticed how much it's really about analogy as the core of cognition. Probably due for a third.
