# Proposal

## Why

Waypoints has no license. The workspace manifest says `Apache-2.0` at `Cargo.toml:36` and every crate inherits it, but no license text exists anywhere in the tree, no source file carries a notice, the README stub and `docs/CONTRIBUTING.md` say nothing about terms, and nothing records what a contributor grants when a pull request lands. The repository has been public since 2026-10-29, so every day without a stated license is a day the Apache-2.0 field is the only thing a reader has to go on — and it is not the license the project means to carry.

What the project means to carry has been decided, and this change writes it down and enforces it. Three goals, in priority order, set the shape:

1. **Protect the hosted sync endpoint.** The sync Station is accept-only, client-driven, and blind to content. Anyone who hosts a modified Station has users interacting with it over a network, which is exactly the condition the GNU Affero General Public License adds in its section 13: the modified Station's source must be offered to those users. AGPL does not forbid hosting; it removes secret differentiation from hosting a modified build. The escape valve for a business that wants that differentiation is a commercial license from the project, the shape MongoDB and Grafana used before each tightened further.
2. **Stay shippable through app stores.** The GPL family conflicts with the Apple App Store's terms (added restrictions, platform DRM, the "Installation Information" demand of section 6). The fix is an additional permission under AGPL section 7, granted to every recipient, that excuses exactly those conflicts and nothing else — and consolidated rights under a contributor agreement, so no single contributor can enforce the conflict against the project's own store listing.
3. **Foreclose the rug-pull while keeping dual licensing.** A contributor license agreement plus AGPL is the power structure that lets a steward sell commercial exceptions; it is also the structure that let other projects flip to source-available terms. The agreement here is deliberately asymmetric in contributors' favour: the Steward may relicense only to Open Source Initiative-approved terms or sell commercial exceptions, every contribution must stay available under the Project License forever, and the grants are _conditioned_ on that — so a flip to a non-open license would be copyright infringement, not a breach of contract.

The ethos these serve is already public on the site and binds every decision below: self-hosting is always fine; hosting unmodified builds is fine; forks are allowed and rebrand (trademark, not copyright, handles confusion); there is no proprietary core behind an open wrapper; revenue is availability, not capability.

## What Changes

- **One license for the whole tree: `AGPL-3.0-or-later`**, set once in `[workspace.package]` and inherited by every first-party crate; the in-repo Nuxt frontend under `src/` is one work with the binary and carries the same terms, and so does first-party developer tooling under `tooling/`. No crate is permissive today. The four sync crates (`outpost-protocol`, `outpost-fabric`, `outpost-hold`, `outpost`) are never a permissive candidate: the protocol crate carries the access-control fold, key schedule, validation and reconciliation — the hard majority of a clean-room Station — so a permissive copy of it would arm exactly the competitor goal 1 exists to constrain. The `bedrock` family stays internal under the same terms. **BREAKING (for any downstream relying on the Apache-2.0 field):** no version of Waypoints was ever released under Apache-2.0, and the field is corrected forward-only; history is not rewritten.
- **The license is a bundle: the verbatim AGPLv3 text plus an App Store additional permission.** `LICENSE` at the root holds the AGPLv3 text exactly as the Free Software Foundation publishes it in plain text, never edited — the FSF permits verbatim copying only, and an edited text may not be called the AGPL. It is the only license file at the root, because GitHub's license detection reads root files only, does not follow symlinks, and matches the plain text exactly. Everything else lives under `docs/licensing/`, which is authored to render as pages: `LICENSE.md`, the FSF's own Markdown edition of the same text, equally verbatim; `COPYING.exceptions`, holding the section 7 permission (full text in design.md, Appendix A); and the contributor agreement. The permission lets anyone convey the app through a third-party store whose non-negotiable terms conflict with the license, on two conditions: comply with everything the store does not prevent, and keep the same version's Corresponding Source available under the plain license through a channel the store does not control. It runs to every recipient, forks included; downstream may strip it from their own copies. The "or any later version" election lives in the notices, not in either edition of the text.
- **Every source file says what license it is under.** Crate entry points, the app entry, and the README carry the full notice (the GNU appendix form plus one line pointing at `docs/licensing/COPYING.exceptions`); every other source file carries the two SPDX tags in its initial comment block. Generated files get their tags from the generator. Third-party code in the tree is exempt because it is not ours to relabel.
- **Third-party code in the tree keeps its own license, and that fact is declared, not inherited.** `tooling/gh-workflow` is a vendored copy of an Apache-2.0 project and currently inherits the workspace license field — after the flip that would label Tailcall's code AGPL. It declares `Apache-2.0` itself and gains the upstream license and notice texts. The two `[patch.crates-io]` forks under `tooling/patches/` are MIT and gain the MIT text their directories lack today. One registry, `.config/third-party.toml`, records every vendored crate, copied file and ported portion with its origin, holder, license and license texts; the manifest check, the header check's exemptions and the notices all derive from it, so a routine ported into a first-party file is attributed too.
- **Dependency licenses are gated and noticed.** The existing `cargo-deny` allowlist — the set of licenses compatible with AGPLv3 that the tree already relies on — moves from a pre-push hook into a pull-request check. The frontend bundle gets the same gate inside the Vite build, with the same allowlist. A `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` covering both ecosystems is generated, committed, kept in lockstep by the existing generated-files check, bundled as an application resource reachable from Settings, and included in release archives — the MIT, ISC and BSD licenses require notice preservation in binary distributions, and today nothing preserves them.
- **One validator, `cargo xtask license`, holds the invariants** that neither `cargo-deny` nor a formatter checks: every first-party manifest's license is a valid, non-deprecated SPDX expression equal to the policy; every header-bearing file's SPDX expression equals its owning manifest's; no permissive workspace crate has a copyleft workspace crate in its normal dependency graph (vacuous today, so a future permissive crate cannot be born wrong); both editions of the license text hash to the FSF's published files; and the frontend allowlist equals the Rust one. It runs on every pull request and in the pre-push hook.
- **Contributors are told the terms and what they grant.** `docs/licensing/README.md` is the long-form overview the website renders: the license and the bundle, the enforcement-intent statement in the Steward's voice, the header rule, the plugin-boundary note, and the pledge — which _quotes_ the contributor agreement's outbound clause rather than promising beside it. `docs/CONTRIBUTING.md` gets one or two paragraphs that name the license and the agreement and link to the texts and the overview, and otherwise defers to them. `docs/licensing/CLA.md` holds the agreement (full text in design.md, Appendix B): Harmony HA-CLA-I 1.0 base; perpetual availability under the Project License as an obligation; OSI-approved relicensing as a bounded permission; commercial exceptions subordinate to the availability obligation; the grants expressly conditioned on all three; a contributor termination right with downstream survival; defensive-only patent revocation; and an ordered Steward succession — designation by declaration, then an acceptance chain on death or incapacity, then a lapse of the relicensing and commercial permissions with every downstream license surviving — with `docs/licensing/STEWARD.md` as the public record of who the Steward is and whether a designation exists. Contributions that carry material the contributor does not own follow a section of the overview the agreement points to: identified in the pull request and at the site, on the allowlist, noticed, and registered; undirected AI output is not a Contribution. Every counsel flag in the drafts is carried as written; this change resolves no legal question.
- **Assent is recorded on every pull request by CLA assistant, against the repository's agreement.** The service the Steward has already configured posts a status check per pull request and re-asks signers whenever its Gist changes. The repository stays the source of truth: the validator compares the Gist with `docs/licensing/CLA.md` and the signing form's field schema on every pull request, and `cargo xtask license publish` — the same comparison plus one authenticated write — pushes the repository's text into the Gist when the Steward runs it after a version bump. Gists is a user-scoped permission the App's installation token cannot reach, so the write runs on the Steward's machine, authenticating as `wp-botswain` through the device flow with no stored credential, never in CI; a forgotten publication or a hand edit to the Gist fails the check. Signers give a name and email, confirm they are at least eighteen, and confirm agreement; nothing more. No OpenTofu provider manages a Gist, which is why nothing is declared there (design.md D9).
- **Every source file names its copyright holder and license.** The holder is John Muchovej; a file's copyright year is the year it was first authored and never changes; the initial comment block of every first-party source file carries at least one `SPDX-FileCopyrightText` line and one `SPDX-License-Identifier: AGPL-3.0-or-later` line, and contributors may add their own copyright line beside the Steward's.
- **Media has a default and a short menu.** Anything bundled into the application binary carries the Project License. The contributor agreement lets the Steward place contributed non-code material under the Project License, `CC-BY-4.0`, `CC-BY-SA-4.0`, `CC0-1.0`, or, for fonts only, the SIL Open Font License 1.1; the Creative Commons options are for documentation and the website, not for bundled assets. Governing law is Connecticut for now.
- **Blanks only the Steward can fill are surfaced, not invented**: the succession window and fallback organisation in the agreement's chain (which gate the agreement's first publication, not the merge), the enforcement-intent statement's wording, and which non-code material — the name, logo, icons, marketing copy — is excluded from the project license.

## Capabilities

No existing domain fits this change, so it mints one: **`licensing`**, a tree. `toolchain` governs how the repository builds, tests and formats itself and may neither constrain app behaviour nor cite product domains — but the license choice rests on what the sync Station is, and the notices file ships inside the app. `distribution` governs packaging and delivery of built artifacts, which the notices file touches only as cargo. Licensing is a legal posture over the whole tree with its own invariants, enforcement and documents; it is as cohesive a boundary as `toolchain` and as orthogonal to the product domains.

### New Capabilities

- `licensing/project-license`: what the project's license is and how it is declared — the uniform policy and its inheritance through the workspace, the license files and where each lives, where the "or later" election is made, which code is never a permissive candidate, the birth policy and layering rule any future permissive crate must satisfy, how generated code, tooling and in-repo surfaces are classified, and that ambiguity resolves to the default.
- `licensing/source-notices`: the notice every source file carries — the full form and where it is required, the short SPDX form everywhere else, which files are header-bearing and which are exempt and why, that generated files get their header from the generator, and that header, manifest and policy are checked for agreement on every pull request.
- `licensing/third-party-code`: how code the project did not write is handled — the compatible-license allowlist for Rust and frontend dependencies and the rule for clarifying bad metadata, in-tree third-party code declaring and carrying its own license, modified weak-copyleft files staying under their license, and the third-party notices artifact: what it covers, that it is generated and lockstep-checked, and where it ships.
- `licensing/contribution`: what a contributor grants and is owed — the agreement's load-bearing clauses as invariants (availability obligation, bounded relicensing, subordinate commercial exception, condition, remedies, succession), the pledge quoting the clause, the enforcement-intent statement, the header rule as contributor policy, and recorded, versioned assent on every pull request.

### Modified Capabilities

None. `distribution/nix-overlay` packages gain `meta.license`, `distribution/release-publishing` archives gain a file, and `toolchain/workflow-generation`'s generator emits one more job — all implementation under requirements that already hold. `toolchain/generated-bindings` already requires every committed generated file to be lockstep-checked; the notices file is one more such file.

## Impact

- **Root:** `LICENSE`, the FSF's plain-text AGPLv3, the only license file there.
- **`docs/licensing/`:** `LICENSE.md`, the FSF's Markdown edition of AGPLv3; `COPYING.exceptions`, the section 7 permission; `CLA.md`, the agreement; `STEWARD.md`, the public record of the current Steward and any succession designation; `README.md`, the long-form overview (bundle, enforcement intent, pledge, header rule, the rules for material a contributor does not own, plugin boundary).
- **`.config/third-party.toml`:** the registry of in-tree third-party code — vendored crates, copied files, ported portions — from which the header exemptions, the manifest check's third-party set and the notices all derive.
- **`docs/README.md`:** replaces the two one-line stubs `README.md` and `.github/README.md`, and carries the license paragraph linking `LICENSE`, `docs/licensing/LICENSE.md`, `docs/licensing/COPYING.exceptions`, `docs/licensing/CLA.md` and `docs/CONTRIBUTING.md`. **`Cargo.toml`:** `[workspace.package] license = "AGPL-3.0-or-later"`, `readme = "docs/README.md"`; `publish = false` stays.
- **`tooling/gh-workflow/`:** `license = "Apache-2.0"` in place of `license.workspace = true`; `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` from upstream; README provenance table and `just gh-workflow-sync` keep them across a sync.
- **`tooling/patches/specta-macros/`, `tooling/patches/specta-serde/`:** `LICENSE-MIT` from upstream specta.
- **Every first-party source file** (`.rs`, `.ts`, `.vue`, `.js`, `.mjs`, `.css`, `.toml`, `.nix`, `.typ`, `.nu`, shell scripts under `tooling/bin/`): the two SPDX tags, with the year each file was first authored; crate `lib.rs` files, `src-tauri/src/lib.rs`, the four `src-tauri/src/bin/*.rs`, `src/app/app.vue` and `docs/README.md` get the full notice. Exempt: `tooling/gh-workflow/`, `tooling/patches/`, `src-tauri/gen/`, lockfiles, assets, and `.d.ts`.
- **`tooling/xtask`:** a `license` task (`check` default; `notices` and `publish` subcommands) on the `spdx` and `cargo_metadata` crates and the GitHub client xtask already has; `typegen` emits the SPDX tags after its generated-file line; `workflows/` emits a `license` job in the Tests workflow and adds `Cargo.lock`, `pnpm-lock.yaml` and `.config/third-party.toml` as sources and the notices file as an output of `check_generated`.
- **`docs/licensing/cla-metadata.json`:** the CLA-assistant signing-form schema (name, email, an at-least-eighteen checkbox, the agreement checkbox), published to the Gist beside the agreement by `cargo xtask license publish` on each version bump.
- **`src/nuxt.config.ts`:** `rollup-license-plugin` with `unacceptableLicenseTest` inverted to the allowlist and `licenseOverrides` for bad metadata; the allowlist constant is checked against `.config/deny.toml` by the validator.
- **`.config/about.toml`** (cargo-about, allowlist mirroring `deny.toml`) and `.config/about.hbs`; `src-tauri/resources/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` committed; `src-tauri/Tauri.toml` gains `bundle.resources` for it and a `copyright` value; a Settings entry under `src/app/pages/settings/app/` renders it.
- **`justfile`:** a `license` recipe running `cargo xtask license check` and `cargo deny check licenses`, the one entry point the hook and CI both call. **`.config/hk.pkl`:** a `license` step running it; **`.config/deny.toml`:** unchanged allowlist, possibly new `[[licenses.clarify]]` entries as cargo-about surfaces missing texts.
- **`nix/packages/waypoints/package.nix`, `nix/packages/outpost/package.nix`:** `meta.license = lib.licenses.agpl3Plus`.
- **`docs/CONTRIBUTING.md`:** a one- or two-paragraph licensing section that links out; **`AGENTS.md`:** one line pointing at the overview (the header rule applies to agents too).
- **`devenv.nix`:** `cargo-about` added to the tool set.
- **CI:** the Tests workflow gains one job; `check-generated` gains three sources and one output. No workflow touches the Gist: `cargo xtask license publish` runs on the Steward's machine, authenticating as the `wp-botswain` App through the device flow, with no stored credential. The pre-push hook gains one step.

## Non-goals

- **No REUSE adoption.** REUSE's value is machine-checking a mixed-license map; at a uniform policy that value is nil, and the headers are already SPDX-formatted, so adopting it later (a `LICENSES/` directory and a root `REUSE.toml`) is additive, not a rewrite. The header check here is the stand-in for `reuse lint`.
- **No permissive tier.** Permissive is a _birth_ policy: a crate may be born `MIT OR Apache-2.0` only if third parties must link it to interoperate, and no existing crate qualifies. AGPL to permissive is a free later flip while copyright is consolidated; the reverse cannot recall shipped versions. The layering check lands now so it is already live when the first such crate appears. `MIT` alone is never used for one (no patent grant); `Apache-2.0` alone is never used (GPLv2-incompatible). Developer tooling is not a permissive tier either: it is never conveyed, so the copyleft on it constrains nobody, and a permissive `xtask` would need the layering rule narrowed because it links `cartograph` and `domain` (design.md, project-license decisions).
- **No content-and-brand carve-out yet.** The website's code is first-party and carries the project license like the app's frontend; that is not a question. What the root license does not fit is the non-code material in the tree — the Waypoints name and logo, the app icons and marketing renders, the website's copy — which under AGPL anyone may reuse, since trademark stops passing-off but not copying. Which of it is excluded from the license grant, with a trademark notice, and whether prose documentation takes `CC-BY-SA-4.0`, is a Steward decision listed in design.md; until then it sits under the root `LICENSE` by default.
- **No legal documents beyond the license.** `docs/licensing/` is authored so its pages can be published on the website; a wider `docs/legal/` tree — terms of service, privacy policy and the like — is separate work and not touched here. Nothing in this change depends on how or when pages are published.
- **No trademark filing, no commercial license terms, no counsel review, no filled CLA blanks.** Steward and counsel work. Every `[⚖ …]` flag in the appendices is carried verbatim and none is resolved.
- **No history rewrite** of the Apache-2.0 field or anything else. Rewrites cannot recall conveyed copies.
- **No entity (corporate) CLA.** A known follow-up; the individual agreement is what lands.
- **No plugin-boundary statement beyond a placeholder.** A Classpath-exception-style clarification that programs communicating with Waypoints over its sync protocol are not derivative works on that basis is wanted. It lands first as the Steward's stated interpretation in the licensing overview, which needs no counsel; upgrading it to a formal section 7 permission that travels with the code is counsel's work, later.
- **No rejected alternatives reopened:** SSPL, BUSL or FSL for the core; LGPL; MPL-2.0 for the core; plain GPL-3.0; DCO only; `AGPL-3.0-only`; a non-objection waiver or alternative-license conveyance in place of the section 7 permission.
