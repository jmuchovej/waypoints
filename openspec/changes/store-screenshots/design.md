## Decisions

### D1 — `frameit` is used for nothing

What `frameit` is wanted for is a realistic picture of a running app, and that is `snapshot` and `screengrab`'s half of the job, not `frameit`'s. `frameit` only frames, titles and pads an image that already exists.

It cannot do that half for us anyway. Its newest Pixel frame is the Pixel 5, five generations behind the Pixel 10 Pro this change targets, and its Android set is frozen around 2020–21; the 2026 refresh that added iPhone 16 and 17 frames added no Android device. And where it could help, it duplicates a layer we own: `screen()` already paints the background and sets the headline, to a design — SW→NE gradient, `em`-scaled type, per-surface `band`/`card` proportions — that `Framefile.json` cannot express.

So fastlane's capture tools are in; its composition tool is out.

### D2 — Frames are sourced renders, kept in the tree and out of history (revised 2026-10-28)

Each screenshot is a composition — headline and supporting line, background, layout — with a capture of the app embedded in a device frame. The frame is a Mockuuups Studio render of the real device, exported straight-on under the commercial licence a paid plan carries, keyed once so the screen is transparent, and placed under `src-tauri/fastlane/res/`.

An earlier draft drew the silhouette instead, on the grounds that no Pixel 10 Pro frame existed to source. That was true of `frameit`'s set and false in general: Mockuuups has both devices, from one vendor, so the two listings look like one product — which was the real reason for drawing both, once a sourced iPhone beside a drawn Pixel was ruled out.

**What the exports are.** Mockuuups fills the screen with opaque black on export, so each frame is exported with a white screen background and the white is keyed out by a flood fill from the screen's centre — not a global colour swap, because the bezel highlights are also pure white. The recipe is in `src-tauri/fastlane/AGENTS.md`. Measured on the exports in hand:

| device        | export    | screen rect (w×h at x,y) | corner radius | against native |
| ------------- | --------- | ------------------------ | ------------- | -------------- |
| iPhone 17 Pro | 1317×2716 | 1202×2619 at 58,49       | 222 px        | 1206×2622 — 1× |
| Pixel 10 Pro  | 2781×5854 | 2519×5615 at 121,120     | 338 px        | 1280×2856 — 2× |

So a capture drops into the iPhone frame at native scale and into the Pixel frame at twice it, and both aspects match the device to three decimals.

**Why out of history.** The licence permits editing a mockup and incorporating it into marketing material, apps and online platforms with no attribution (§4.2, §4.3), and exports outlive the subscription (§1.2, §6). It prohibits redistributing a mockup standalone, modified or not (§5, §5.1). A keyed frame PNG committed to a repository that could one day be published is exactly a standalone copy, so the frames are never committed: `**/*.png` already ignores them, `res/.gitkeep` says what belongs there, and whoever renders obtains the exports under their own plan. Separately, Apple's and Google's rights in their own hardware are not the vendor's to license and apply to any render from any source; both stores' practice tolerates device frames, and that question is noted for legal review rather than settled here.

**What `devices.typ` holds.** Per device: the image path, the screen rect, the corner radius and the capture scale. No drawn geometry and no colour — `brand-palette` is not engaged, because a product render is not one of our colours. Composition layers the capture under the frame: clip the capture to the rounded screen rect, then draw the frame over it, so the Dynamic Island and the punch hole occlude the capture the way the hardware does. Simulator screenshots include the pixels behind the island, so the status bar shows either side of it as on a real phone.

### D3 — A link names a route, not a scene

A capture must show the same thing every run, which means a configured territory and no real user data. Nearly all of that already exists: `store/mock` seeds the `getting-started` Bundle through `transload`, the same ingest an import takes. How that corpus reaches a release build is D3c; this decision is about reaching a particular screen once it has.

Getting to a _particular_ screen needs something more. The harness cannot navigate there: both XCUITest and Espresso address native accessibility elements, so a Tauri app's UI reaches them only as a flattened, unstable projection of the DOM, and a screenshot harness that breaks on unrelated markup changes is worse than no harness. The app has to be told where to go, and it already has a mechanism for being told things from outside: `tauri-plugin-deep-link`, a registered scheme, and a frontend handler that classifies a URL and routes on it.

**The link's path is the app's route.** `waypoints://library`, `waypoints://marks/<id>`, `waypoints://settings/sync` — the routes under `src/app/pages/` as they already exist, not a parallel vocabulary of capture scenes. There is nothing to register and nothing to keep in step: a route that exists is linkable, and the capture harness is one caller among whatever else wants to link into the app.

This is what makes the feature worth more than the screenshots. A scene registry would have been scaffolding that only the harness used; routes are the app's own surface.

Two things nothing else currently exercises:

**The custom scheme on mobile is unproven.** `[plugins.deep-link.desktop]` registers `schemes = ["waypoints"]`, and `register_all()` in `src-tauri/src/lib.rs` registers at runtime — both desktop paths. The mobile side, `[[plugins.deep-link.mobile]]`, carries `host = "waypoints.so"` and `pathPrefix = ["/pair"]`: universal and app links, not the custom scheme. So a scheme link on a phone is untested, and making capture depend on it tests it on every capture run. The plugin's `scheme` key should be enough — it generates `CFBundleURLTypes` on iOS and the intent filter on Android — but that is a researched claim, not yet a demonstrated one.

**A second link kind forces the dispatch to become one.** `src/app/plugins/deep-link.client.ts` funnels every incoming URL through `classifySetupCode` and `continue`s on `unknown`; pairing is not one kind among several, it is the only path. A route link would be dropped on the floor today. Making it work means the handler dispatches on kind first, which is the additive property worth having.

The cost is that this change touches the pairing path. The constraint that keeps it safe is D3b.

### D3a — The scheme is flavor-scoped

`[plugins.deep-link.desktop]` lives in the base `Tauri.toml`, so every flavor registers `waypoints://`. With stable, preview and nightly installed side by side — which is the whole point of their separate identifiers — which app receives a `waypoints://pair` link is left to the operating system. That is a latent bug today, not a new one; it simply has not bitten because only one flavor is usually installed.

So the scheme carries the flavor: `waypoints`, `waypoints-preview`, `waypoints-nightly`. The flavor configs under `src-tauri/res/configs/flavors/` already override `[plugins.updater]` for exactly this kind of per-channel difference, so the scheme moves beside it and the base config stops declaring one.

For capture this matters immediately: the harness drives whichever flavor it built, and an ambiguous scheme would make "did the right app open" unanswerable.

**Development takes the stable scheme**, because the alternative is not available. The appealing option — development claims all three and wins only while it is running, yielding to whatever is installed when it is not — is not something the platforms offer:

- Scheme registration is **persistent, not process-scoped**. It writes the executable's absolute path as the handler; that is why `unregister` exists and why a moved AppImage invalidates its own link. A development build that registered on launch would still own the scheme after it exited.
- **macOS cannot register at runtime at all.** It is grouped with iOS and Android as config-only, and `register` returns `UnsupportedPlatform` there. So even a register-on-start/unregister-on-exit approximation would work on Windows and Linux only, and would leave the scheme hijacked after any crash that skipped the unregister.
- **Nothing prefers a running instance across applications.** Single-instance forwarding routes a link into the running instance of _the same_ app; it does not arbitrate between apps.

So development registers `waypoints`, the same as stable. On a machine with both installed the ambiguity returns, but that machine belongs to whoever built the development copy, and it is the situation that exists today.

One consequence to know rather than fix: `src-tauri/src/lib.rs` calls `register_all()` at startup and logs a warning on failure. On macOS that call cannot succeed, so the warning is expected there and is not a symptom.

### D3b — A link navigates; it never acts

A deep link may route the app somewhere. It may not perform the thing that route would do.

This generalises a rule the pairing path already follows — a setup code arrives prefilled in the onboarding input and is never auto-submitted, because "adopting an identity is not something a URL should do on its own". Stating it once covers every kind added later, and it is what makes route links safe in a release build: `waypoints://library` opening the library is navigation, and navigation is not a capability worth gating.

Which is why there is no capture-only gate on these links. An earlier draft proposed one, on the reasoning that a link into fixture content is a capability; that reasoning dissolves once the link names a route rather than a scene, because the fixture content is a returning user's data and the route is one the user can already reach.

### D3d — Presentation state is URL-addressable; this change lands the convention and one pose (2026-10-28)

A route says which page. It does not say what the page shows of itself — which Mark is selected, which panel is open, which tab — and that is what a listing screenshot almost always needs. Today none of it is in the URL: nothing under `src/app/pages/` reads `route.query`, and the only `query` in `src/app` is the command menu's text filter. A link can reach a page but cannot pose it.

Choosing poses is content work and belongs to the next change. Being able to express one is mechanism, and it is this change's for the same reason route links are: it is the app's own surface, not harness scaffolding, and anything else that wants to open the app in a state gets it for free. Two parts:

- **The handler forwards the whole URL.** Path, params, query and hash go to the router unchanged. The handler classifies the kind and otherwise does not interpret a route link.
- **One convention for reading presentation state from the route**, so every page poses the same way: a page declares the query keys it honours and reads them through one composable; undeclared keys are ignored; nothing is ever written back. The convention is documented beside the handler. Its name and shape are implementation.

Proven once. The demo screen, `/library`, carries one pose — chosen during implementation as whatever the library can show that the bare route cannot — and the demo capture is taken through it. The catalogue of which pages honour which keys rides with the content change, page by page, because a key no screen uses is scaffolding.

D3b bounds it: a pose is presentation. Selecting a Mark or opening a panel is navigation; creating, editing or deleting anything is not a pose, and no key may do it.

### D3c — The demo territory is placed on the device; the app is not built differently (2026-10-23)

The corpus has to reach a release build, and a release build seeds nothing: `collections::getting_started::run` sits behind `store/mock`, which only `development` enables. Three ways in were weighed.

- **Build a seeded copy.** A second build with `mock` on — the earlier draft. Rejected: the screenshot would depict a binary nobody installs, and on Android that is gratuitous, because the release apk installs on the emulator as it is.
- **Pair to a Station holding the demo territory.** The release build onboards as a returning user does: pairing link, confirm, library fills. It would exercise onboarding on mobile the way route links exercise the scheme. Deferred, not rejected: notes do not sync yet — identity and pairing do, `Maneuver` ops never become Notices — so a paired build arrives with an empty library, which the spec refuses to capture. It would also need a hosted Station, and would put a demo identity's recovery phrase wherever capture runs, since the pairing payload carries it.
- **Place the territory on disk before first launch.** The store is a bedrock database at one path under the app's data directory (`crates/store/src/store.rs:32`). `cargo xtask demo-territory` opens such a store on the host with `mock` on and ingests a Bundle through the path the seeder already uses — `getting-started` by default, any Bundle by argument, so a corpus written for the listing is a later input rather than a later mechanism; the harness installs the release build, copies the result into the app's container — a host-visible directory on the simulator, `adb root` on a non-Play emulator image — and only then launches. The app finds a returning user's data and opens onto it. Nothing is compiled differently, nothing crosses a network, no secret is held, and the content is there on first paint.

The third is the mechanism. The seeder is the same code either way; what moves is where it runs — host rather than app — and the schema is the commit's own because the same crates produce and open it. The harness never authors content: copying files is placement, which is why the spec says the territory is _brought to_ the build rather than _comes from_ it. When content sync lands, the second option replaces this one and the requirement holds as written — still the demo territory, still brought to a release build, by then through the front door.

**What gets installed.** On Android, the release apk itself: it is universal (`aarch64-linux-android` and `x86_64-linux-android` are both declared targets and `xtauri build android` passes every one), so the same bytes Play would receive install on an x86_64 emulator. On iOS, a simulator build of the same commit and flavor, because nothing we control can run the ipa: it is `iphoneos` arm64 and distribution-signed, the simulator loads `iphonesimulator` builds only, and an App Store–signed ipa cannot be sideloaded onto a device either. Same flavor, same configuration, same commit — only the SDK differs, and that is the one place "a build of the commit" is weaker than "the build".

### D4 — Minting is manual; the pipeline is not touched (revised 2026-10-28)

Earlier drafts put capture in the release pipeline — a job beside each build, an artifact handoff, the listing kept in the release store, nightly as the canary. All of that is withdrawn from this change. What remains is the minting process itself, run by hand: `xfastlane capture <platform>` on a developer's machine, producing captures the existing `xfastlane build` composes into `metadata/<platform>/`.

Two reasons, both about not deciding things this change cannot yet know.

**Automating it needs a tracking policy first, and that policy is open.** Whether captures are committed turns on how often they should change, and that differs by channel: nightly churns daily, so committing its captures inflates the repository for nothing; preview and stable change rarely, so committing theirs is plausible and keeps the listing reproducible from a checkout; or captures are minted when a release group is cut and refreshed with the group, so the listing tracks the cadence of what users see rather than the cadence of builds. Each of those gives the pipeline a different job — regenerate, read from the tree, or read from wherever a group's captures are kept — and the pipeline cannot be designed before one is chosen. That choice belongs to a change of its own; this one keeps captures as generated output under the existing `**/*.png` rule and says nothing about their future.

**The cost is unknown until the process has been run.** Simulator and emulator minutes, how long a territory takes to place, how often the harness flakes — none of it has been measured, and a pipeline designed around guesses is redesigned once. Running it by hand for a while is the measurement.

What the pipeline does meanwhile is unchanged: `publish` runs `prepare`, which renders from whatever captures are in the tree — today none, which is why the demo screen is the only one ported (task 5.6) and why no listing ships between this change and the first real release. A referenced capture that is missing still fails the render by name (task 5.4), so a listing with a hole in it cannot be rendered by accident, in CI or anywhere else.

### D5 — The capture's canvas is never the store's canvas

Neither device's native capture matches its upload slot:

|               | capture          | store slot           |
| ------------- | ---------------- | -------------------- |
| iPhone 17 Pro | 1206×2622        | 1320×2868 (6.9-inch) |
| Pixel 10 Pro  | 1280×2856 (20:9) | 1080×1920 (9:16)     |

A raw capture is therefore never uploadable, and compositing is required however the frames are done — which is the pipeline's existing shape, not a new cost. The capture goes inside the frame, the frame onto the surface's canvas.

The mismatch between the iPhone 17 Pro and the 6.9-inch slot it is composed onto carries no fidelity cost: the canvas is ours, and inside it the capture sits in its own device's frame at native scale (iPhone) or half scale (Pixel), never stretched to the slot. Both devices were chosen as the current flagships, which is what a listing should look like it was taken on.

### D6 — `capture` is a nushell subcommand of `xfastlane`; `demo-territory` is an xtask

`xfastlane build` renders; `xfastlane capture <platform>` boots a device, installs the build, places the demo territory, runs the harness and writes captures. Keeping them apart is what lets `build` stay runnable anywhere while `capture` is allowed to need a simulator, an emulator, and minutes.

Where each half lives follows the split the tooling already has. Capture is orchestration of command-line tools — `simctl`, `adb`, `emulator`, `fastlane snapshot` and `screengrab` — none of which has a Rust API worth wrapping, and `xfastlane` already is the nushell script that wraps `fastlane`; a `capture` subcommand beside `build`, `metadata` and `prepare` is the obvious home. Producing the territory is the opposite case: it opens a store through `crates/store` with `mock` on, which is Rust-native and belongs in `xtask`, so `capture` calls `cargo xtask demo-territory` the way other scripts call `xtask` for anything that touches the crates.

On Android `capture` takes the release apk it should install (`--package`), building one with `xtauri build android` when none is given; on iOS it builds the simulator variant itself, since no artifact can supply one. The territory is copied in before the first launch — `capture` fails rather than launching if the store it placed is not where the app will look.

## Open questions

None outstanding for this change. Closed along the way:

- **Whether the custom scheme reaches a Tauri mobile build through config alone.** It should: `[[plugins.deep-link.mobile]]` takes a `scheme` key for both iOS and Android, and the plugin generates the `CFBundleURLTypes` entries; only App Links and Universal Links need a `host` and a `.well-known` file. Task 1.4 demonstrates it.
- **Which route the demo capture shows.** `/library`.
